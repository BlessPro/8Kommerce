## Portfolio summary

### 8Kommerce — making ecommerce accessible to small businesses

8Kommerce is a mobile-first commerce platform that helps Ghanaian small
businesses manage products, orders, stock, payments, delivery and customer
tracking in one place.

Research and usability conversations with approximately 27 participants showed
that many sellers repeatedly sent product images to individual customers and
managed the buying journey inside separate chats. Important order details and
useful business data could easily be lost.

As founder, product designer and full-stack developer, I led the project from
research and product strategy through interface design, Laravel engineering,
security, testing and production deployment.

The resulting private-beta platform includes phone-and-PIN seller access,
custom storefronts, inventory reservations, Paystack checkout, configurable
delivery and pickup, bulk order management, customer SMS and tracking, seller
insights, referrals and promoter attribution.

The production release is supported by 162 automated tests with 950 assertions,
Docker-based releases, queue isolation, health checks and deployment backups.
The next stage is measuring seller activation, checkout conversion, fulfilment
time and retention with a controlled group of real businesses.

## LinkedIn launch post

I started 8Kommerce with one goal: make ecommerce more accessible to small
businesses and help them grow with real data.

Through research and usability conversations with approximately 27 people, I
saw how sellers repeatedly sent product images to individual customers and
managed orders inside separate chats. Products, customer decisions, payment
proof and delivery details could easily become disconnected.

I designed and built 8Kommerce to bring that journey into one mobile-first
system: storefronts, products, stock, checkout, delivery, customer tracking and
business insights.

As founder, product designer and full-stack developer, I worked across product
strategy, UX, interface design, Laravel engineering, payment and SMS workflows,
security, automated testing, CI/CD and production deployment.

The platform is now live as a private beta. The current release is backed by
162 automated tests with 950 assertions, reproducible Docker releases and a
secured production environment.

The next stage is the most important one: learning from real sellers and using
their results to shape what comes next.

## Investor presentation version

Use six slides:

1. **Problem:** social sellers operate across chats, screenshots and manual
   records.
2. **Insight:** they do not only need a website; they need connected commerce
   operations.
3. **Solution:** mobile-first storefront, checkout, stock, fulfilment, tracking
   and insight.
4. **Evidence:** approximately 27 research participants and a production-ready
   private beta.
5. **Defensibility:** connected operational data, localized workflows and
   reliability infrastructure.
6. **Next milestone:** controlled seller activation and measurable transaction
   growth.

Do not present 162 tests as market traction. It is evidence of engineering
readiness. Market traction should use real seller, order, conversion and
retention data once available.

## PDF recommendations

- Target 10–14 pages.
- Use one major idea per page.
- Keep body copy short and move technical depth into captions.
- Use real screenshots rather than generic device mockups everywhere.
- Annotate important interactions with one-sentence explanations.
- Include one research page, one architecture page and one trade-off page.
- Finish with measured outcomes and next steps.
- Add a link or QR code to the live product only when public access is intended.

Remove personal phone numbers, customer details, provider keys, payment
references and real private records before publishing screenshots.

## Evidence rules

- Use “approximately 27 participants” unless a confirmed participant record
  provides a more exact number.
- Do not invent conversion rates, revenue, seller counts or customer outcomes.
- Label targets as targets, not results.
- Separate product readiness from market traction.
- Update the case study after the private beta produces verified metrics.
